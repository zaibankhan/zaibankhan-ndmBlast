About BLAST & The Alignment Methods

What is BLAST?

BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool) is used to compare an unknown query sequence (nucleotide or protein) against a database of known sequences to find the most similar matches. This application compares your sequence against a local database of NDM (New Delhi Metallo-β-lactamase) variants.

Available Alignment Methods

On the BLASTN / BLASTP pages you can choose how the query is aligned to each database sequence:

  • Smith-Waterman (Local Alignment): Finds the optimal local region of similarity allowing gaps (insertions / deletions), using dynamic programming. This is the recommended, more accurate method for finding conserved regions and handling sequences of different lengths.
  • Simple (Global / same-offset comparison): Compares both sequences position-by-position from the start, without introducing gaps. This is the original method and is faster but less accurate when indels are present.

Interpreting Results

  • Identity (%): The percentage of matching characters within the aligned region.
  • E-value: An estimate of how many matches you would expect by chance in a database of this size. A lower E-value indicates a more significant match.
  • Alignment: Shows the query and subject sequences with the match line; | = match, * = mismatch, - = gap.
