About BLAST & the Alignment Methods

How this tool works and how to interpret your results.

What is BLAST?

BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool) is used to compare an unknown query sequence (nucleotide or protein) against a database of known sequences to find the most similar matches. This application compares your sequence against a local database of NDM (New Delhi Metallo-β-lactamase) variants stored on your own machine — nothing is sent to any external server.

Available Alignment Methods

Local — Smith-Waterman (recommended)

Finds the optimal region of local similarity while allowing gaps (insertions / deletions), using dynamic programming. Most accurate for identifying conserved regions and for sequences of different lengths.

Global — Simple

A fast comparison that aligns both sequences position-by-position from the start without introducing gaps. Faster for quick screening but less accurate when insertions or deletions are present.

Interpreting Results

  • Identity (%): the percentage of matching characters within the aligned region.
  • E-value: an estimate of how many matches you would expect by chance; a lower E-value means a more significant match.
  • Alignment: shows the query and subject sequences with the match line — | = match, * = mismatch, - = gap.
